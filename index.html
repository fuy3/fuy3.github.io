Yiming Fu

MSc Artificial Intelligence, The University of Manchester

Understanding how intelligent systems perceive, represent, and interact with the world.

My research explores human-centred intelligence, multimodal learning, representation & interpretability, and embodied interaction. I am interested in how multimodal representations align with human perception and cognition, how they can be understood through model behaviour, and how they support interaction and action.

Most recently, I worked with the Cognitive Robotics Laboratory in Manchester on numerosity representations in vision–language models. At Latencore, I build multimodal data pipelines and UMI hardware for robot learning, exploring how additional modalities such as audio can support vision–language–action models. With two years of software engineering experience, I enjoy bringing research questions from analysis into working models and systems.

    Projects

    UMI hardware and audio-augmented VLA

    Latencore, Cambridge, 2026

    Built a Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) rig end to end, from soldering and 3D printing to assembly and debugging, with magnetic encoders, industrial cameras and VR tracking, and used it to collect demonstrations. Introduced an audio modality, checking its validity and interpretability, then built the multimodal fusion framework and trained the models, evaluated on a real robot.

    Hardware build, data collection, multimodal fusion, VLA training

    NumerosityVLM

    Cognitive Robotics Laboratory, The University of Manchester

    A diagnostic benchmark built with orthogonal design and controlled visual cues, plus layer-wise analysis of what models encode about quantity versus what they output.

    10,800 images, 6 conditions, 7 open models

    arXivCodeDataset

    PerceptEEGDraft

    MSc dissertation, The University of Manchester

    Diagnoses where EEG responses and pretrained visual features disagree, then aligns them using frozen OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 targets and a spatiotemporal residual adapter.

    Within subject, R@130.10% → 65.85%
    Within subject, R@591.40%
    Cross subject, R@112.85%
    PDF (draft)VideoProject page

    Knowing when to stop in object-goal navigation

    Cognitive Robotics Laboratory, The University of Manchester; co-author

    Agents that search for an object often fail at the last step, deciding when they are close enough to stop. A learnable depth-aware gate on an Object Memory Transformer adjusts only the stop decision, using explicit depth cues; trained with PPO in AI2-THOR.

    Success rate 70.1% → 80.5%, wrong-stop rate 25.2% → 14.1%

    TAROS 2026 Best Paper Award
    PaperCode and video

    Embroidery pattern generation

    Diffusion models for Miao and Qiang embroidery, 2025

    Clean line drawings extracted from embroidery photos through colour-gradient edges and filtering of unclosed pixels; new patterns generated in a stitch style with a LoRA trained on 21 images and model merging; selected regions recoloured from colour strokes or text.

    Project page
    Diffusion models, LoRA, model fusion, image processing

    iCub object recognition

    Cognitive Robotics and Computer Vision coursework (78). The University of Manchester, 2025

    Handcrafted pipelines (SIFT, ORB and HOG with bag of words), a small CNN and ResNet-18, trained from scratch or pretrained, compared on iCubWorld 1.0 and STL-10 with 40 training images per class. A robot-view test set changes viewpoint and background to expose reliance on background cues.

    Robot view: 50.1% with pretraining vs 18.8% for the small CNN

    PDF
    OpenCV, PyTorch, transfer learning

    Compact residual CNN

    Topics in Machine Learning coursework (full marks). The University of Manchester, 2025

    A compact residual network for 28×28 images: a stem convolution, two residual blocks with strided transitions and a global-average-pooling head. Tuned with Bayesian optimisation under 5-fold cross-validation, with ablations on augmentation, channel width and pooling.

    93.1% on Fashion-MNIST with 67k parameters; 98% on MNIST

    PDF
    PyTorch, Bayesian optimisation, ablation studies

    Lecture+

    VR classroom with an LLM teaching assistant. Final-year project, DCU, 2023–2024

    A Unity (C#) VR lecture hall where lecturers share videos by class code through a Firebase backend, with an assistant built on LLaMA 2 (7B, chat) instruction-tuned on 70 university FAQs as a pilot domain to curb hallucination. The model runs on Hugging Face Inference Endpoints on AWS, with 2.1 s mean and 4.75 s P95 latency under load testing.

    Unity, C#, Firebase, LLaMA 2, AWS

    ScavengerAR

    Gamified museum-guide web app. Team project, DCU, 2022–2023

    A scavenger hunt through a gallery: visitors photograph artworks, image recognition checks each find, and hints, points and a collection board carry the tour, with no account needed. Audience research shaped three paths: a game layer for younger visitors, a simpler interface for older visitors and graded art content for professionals. Tested with 13 recruited users, 93% positive feedback.

    Demo video
    User research, Figma, image recognition, AR
